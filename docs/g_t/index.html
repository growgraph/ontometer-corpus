    How to read it. A link from one ontology to another means it specialises it: it declares classes under the other's classes, that is, it builds on it. The angle is fixed. Each ontology sits in the wedge of the ontology it specialises most, and top-level groups follow reuse communities. The distance from the centre is hyperbolic: r = rmax·(1 − log(1+s)/log N), where s counts the ontologies that build on it, directly or transitively, by that year. N is one more than the largest s at capture, so the leader ends at the centre. Dashed rings mark s = 1, 10 and 100. Filled dots are built on by at least one ontology; hollow dots are not, yet.

    Dates. An ontology appears at its first-evidence date: the earliest of its registry release dates, the first web-archive capture of its IRI and its Wikidata inception date, and never later than the first observed specialisation of its classes. A link appears at the first release that asserts it when the release panel covers the specialiser, and otherwise at the specialiser's first evidence. Registries and the web archive start later than the oldest ontologies, so those are dated late and appear already built on: their leaf phase is not observed.
